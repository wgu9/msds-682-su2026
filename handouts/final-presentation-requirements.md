# MSDS 682 Final Presentation Requirements

The final presentation is worth **20% of the course grade**. Present the
shortest clear explanation of the problem, system, evidence, and result.

The final presentation session is **Thursday, August 13, 2026, from 5:30 to
7:20 PM PDT**. Review the published
[presentation schedule](#/handouts/final-presentation-schedule) for your exact
start time and the
[20-point presentation rubric](#/handouts/final-presentation-rubric) for the
atomic scoring rules.

## Time limits

| Project format | Presentation | Q&A | Total slot | Speaking time per student |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Individual | 5 minutes | 1 minute | **6 minutes** | About 5 minutes |
| Two-person team | 7 minutes | 1 minute | **8 minutes** | About 3–4 minutes each |

Both members of a two-person team must speak. Presentations will stop at the
stated time limit so that every project receives its full slot.

## Required story

Your presentation should make these five points clear:

1. **Problem and target user:** What problem does the project solve, and who
   needs the result?
2. **Data and event contract:** Where does the data come from, and what event
   enters Kafka?
3. **Streaming architecture:** How does data move from the source through Kafka
   to the processor and output?
4. **End-to-end result and evidence:** What useful result did the system
   produce, and how did you validate or evaluate it?
5. **AI element and lessons:** Where did AI add value, how was it checked, and
   what limitation or next step matters most?

## Recommended Five-Slide Pitch

Five slides in an elevator-pitch style are recommended, but the slide count is
not a base grading requirement:

1. Problem and target user.
2. Data source and Kafka event contract.
3. Streaming architecture.
4. Result and validation or evaluation evidence.
5. AI role, verification, lesson, and next step.

Use an equivalent concise structure if it communicates the required story more
clearly for your project.

## Presentation rules

- A live demo is optional. Whether live or recorded, show visible end-to-end
  result and evaluation evidence.
- Do not walk through every file or read large blocks of code.
- Use the one-minute Q&A for one focused audience or instructor question.
- For a two-person team, both students must understand the complete
  architecture, code path, AI usage, and evaluation.

## Extra Credit

- The presentation rubric contains up to **3 presentation-specific bonus
  points** for a design tradeoff, evidence visualization, and technical defense.
- Students may earn up to **2 additional individual points** by completing
  reviews of three other projects under the
  [Peer-Review Extra-Credit Rules](#/handouts/final-presentation-peer-reviews).
- Bonus work cannot replace a missing base requirement.
