Three architecture patterns

The delivered middle matters more than the planned tool count

Better trade: cut one planned layer early and use the saved time for replay, failure handling, tests, documentation, and one believable output.

Not every source was truly live

“Streaming” described several different source patterns

Live external eventsBART GTFS-RT; Finnhub market feedsNear-real-time or real-time source, with the polling/WebSocket boundary stated

Precise language strengthens the project. Historical replay is completely acceptable when the student explains what is simulated and what is genuinely continuous.

Across the cohort

Recurring strengths and recurring risks

What worked

Concrete product thinking

  • Real or believable event source
  • Kafka producer and downstream consumer
  • Visible database, table, dashboard, or web output
  • Architecture tied to a recognizable user problem
What broke trust

Evidence and reproducibility gaps

  • Planned layers described as if they were implemented
  • Core consumer or persistence bridge missing
  • Cloud-only systems without a replay path
  • Secrets, environment folders, or generated state included in submissions
This lecture uses sanitized architecture summaries only. The archived 2023 bundle is professor-only and must not be distributed.

Review every proposal the same way

Five questions reveal the real project

  1. Problem: who uses the result, and what decision changes?
  2. Events: what arrives, at what rate, and what is real versus replayed?
  3. Kafka: what are the topic, key, contract, retention, and replay reason?
  4. State: what join, window, aggregate, dedupe, or current answer must be maintained?
  5. Evidence: what output, metric, failure case, and fallback will be demonstrated?
